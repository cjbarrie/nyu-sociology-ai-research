Twenty-four fictional people experience forty sequential personal concerns. Concerns do not represent equal intervals of time. Without a chatbot, each concern has a 70% probability of producing a request to one of three fixed contacts. The recipient responds with an individual probability between 55% and 95%.

Substitution diverts the selected share of would-be human requests to a chatbot. Of concerns that would not produce a human request, 70% instead go to the available chatbot. The follow-up slider sets the share of chatbot interactions followed by a human request. A chatbot always responds in this model; response quality and wellbeing are not modeled. “No response” means neither a chatbot reply nor a successful human response. A human request can occur without receiving a response.

Both worlds use the same people, recipients and random draws. The highlighted routes correspond to the displayed event; faint routes show earlier human requests. There is no relationship erosion, learning, feedback or competition for a friend's time. Counts are consequences of these assumptions, not estimates of real chatbot effects or long-term social isolation. Follow-up sequences alone would not establish a causal mechanism in observational data.
