Rules, estimands and limits

For each person, support availability is uniform on [0,1]. Without chatbot use, the probability of one human-directed support request on a day is 0.10 + 0.60 × availability. Uptake probability is 0.55 + selection × (0.5 − availability), clipped to [0.01,0.99]. Use requires a randomized offer and a fixed uptake draw below that probability. The effect slider adds to daily request probability for users, also clipped. There is at most one request per day. Shared daily uniform draws produce paired potential outcomes.

The known effect averages each person's difference in requests under an offer versus no offer. ITT compares observed requests across randomized offer groups. The users/non-users contrast conditions on actual uptake and is not generally causal. Randomization need not balance a single small sample. Repeated assignments show sampling variation, not uncertainty about whether these behavioral rules describe reality.

There is no friendship network, spillover, outside chatbot access, attrition, outcome misreporting or relationship change. The visible town is an interface, not a spatial model; avatar motion and appearance carry no analytical meaning. This is a transparent teaching and design sandbox, not evidence about actual chatbot effects. It can be extended to examine proposed estimators before collecting data.
