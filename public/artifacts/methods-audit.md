# Methods audit: a study of chatbot use and informal support-seeking

Independent methods review, 9 September 2026, of the evidence table and version-two memo. No researcher answers are represented.

## The first question I would ask

When you say that chatbots displace human support, do you mean fewer requests for help, fewer successful conversations, or weaker relationships? Those claims require different outcomes. I suggest starting with requests to friends and family; the relationship claim needs longer follow-up.

The current memo correctly separates these outcomes, but its proposed bridge-oriented chatbot experiment would not identify the overall effect of chatbot availability. It would estimate the effect of a particular design intervention relative to its comparator. That distinction should survive into the title and abstract.

## A useful research product

Replace the simulation in the main demonstration with a short study protocol and an accompanying diary instrument. The audience can inspect the primary outcome, assignment scheme, and analysis choices. A coauthor can then challenge an actual decision rather than a result mechanically generated by a simulation.

A feasible pilot would recruit adults for a baseline diary week followed by four follow-up weeks. Randomly assign participants to:

1. Their usual access to existing services.
2. An offer of access to a specified chatbot, with standardized onboarding.
3. The same offer and onboarding, with a feature encouraging contact with a friend or family member when appropriate.

This estimates effects of these offers in a setting where other chatbots remain available. It does not estimate the effect of eliminating all chatbot access. Record outside use and contamination; do not exclude participants because they do not use the assigned service. This pilot still needs a precision target.

## Outcome and estimands

Define the primary outcome as the number of distinct, self-reported requests for emotional support or personal advice initiated toward an existing friend or family member during the fixed four-week follow-up. Count a request whether or not the recipient responds. Distinguish a new episode from repeated messages about one request in the diary instructions.

The primary contrast is the mean count under assignment to the standard offer minus the mean count under usual access: an intention-to-treat effect of the offer. The second contrast compares the bridge-feature offer with the standard offer. Prespecify the testing priority or multiplicity adjustment. Report mean differences with uncertainty; a prespecified adjustment for baseline outreach can improve precision.

Retain zero-request participants. A missing diary is not a zero. Report completion by assignment, specify a missing-data strategy, and assess sensitivity to differential nonresponse. Do not control for post-assignment chatbot minutes, loneliness, or reported needs in the primary analysis. Each may be affected by assignment.

A separate episode diary can describe the order of chatbot and human contact, whether the person received a response, and perceived usefulness. Rates conditional on reported needs are secondary because treatment might alter which needs arise or get reported. Temporal sequences alone do not establish mediation.

## Questions that should change the protocol

- Is the substantive target reduced outreach, or harm? Fewer requests could reflect relief, avoidance, or both. Measure wellbeing separately.
- Which population matters: current companion users, new users, or adults generally? Recruitment determines the interpretation.
- Are self-reports sufficient? Contact metadata cannot establish supportive content.
- Could friends join together? Spillovers would challenge the individual assignment interpretation.
- What difference would matter substantively? That answer should determine the precision target.

The evidence table's Fang entry describes randomized modes and topics alongside self-selected use intensity. A regression on minutes does not inherit the experiment's randomization. None of the listed outcome summaries should be relabeled as a direct count of informal support requests without inspecting its instrument.

## Cross-check with the source auditor

I agree with the source auditor’s denominator objection. Conditioning on post-assignment need episodes can select different episodes across arms if assignment changes experienced or reported need. Keep the fixed-window person-level count primary; episode sequences remain informative secondary descriptions. I also agree that Fang’s mode/topic contrasts cannot answer an access question. Our offer contrast has a different, explicitly limited target; the bridge contrast tests a feature, not chatbots generally.
