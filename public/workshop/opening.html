Cognitive offloading and research agents

Selected primary research and technical reports, checked 9 September 2026. This is a bounded reading list for the opening discussion, not a systematic review or citation ranking.

The studies examine different outcomes: human memory and learning, reported effort, engineering execution, and scientific judgment. Their findings need not contradict one another.

Source and claim record · BibTeX

Risko & Gilbert (2016)

Cognitive Offloading

Review · Trends in Cognitive Sciences

Offloading changes the cognitive demands of a task by using external actions or resources. Its consequences depend on the task and on decisions about when to offload.

Interpretation: A conceptual and empirical review, not evidence that generative AI causes lasting cognitive decline.

Source check

UCL repository abstract; author manuscript search record.

Sparrow, Liu & Wegner (2011)

Google Effects on Memory: Cognitive Consequences of Having Information at Our Fingertips

Experiments · Science

In computer-based memory experiments, expecting later access changed recall of information and its location.

Interpretation: An influential precursor about memory allocation; not an experiment on present-day AI or general intelligence.

Source check

Original abstract and authors’ university account; PubMed full-page retrieval encountered CAPTCHA.

Lee et al. (2025)

The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers

Survey · CHI 2025

319 workers supplied 936 use examples. Greater confidence in AI was associated with less reported critical thinking.

Interpretation: Self-report and association do not establish loss of cognitive ability over time.

Source check

Abstract; sections 4–5 of the primary paper.

Open consulted source

Bastani et al. (2025)

Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics

Randomized field experiment · PNAS

In a Turkish high school, unrestricted GPT help improved practice performance but reduced subsequent unaided exam grades by 17% relative to control. A guided tutor largely removed that penalty.

Interpretation: This identifies an effect in a particular learning setting; it does not establish faculty deskilling or permanent cognitive loss.

Source check

Published PNAS article supplied by the presenter; abstract on page 1. Earlier author-manuscript results were checked during research. August 2025 correction concerns an affiliation, not results: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12403119/

Kosmyna et al. (2025)

Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task

Preprint · arXiv v2, 31 December 2025

Essay-writing study with EEG: 54 participants in the first three sessions and 18 in the fourth; reports differences in connectivity and recall across tool conditions.

Interpretation: EEG differences are not a direct measure of permanent cognitive damage. Small task-specific study; retain its preprint status.

Source check

arXiv abstract and version history. Included because it is prominent in public discussion, not as the strongest causal evidence.

Gottweis et al. (2026)

Accelerating scientific discovery with Co-Scientist

Research article · Nature, published 19 May 2026

Specialized agents generate, critique and refine hypotheses. Selected biomedical hypotheses received laboratory evaluation, including drug-repurposing candidates tested in vitro.

Interpretation: Scientists selected and tested hypotheses. These demonstrations do not establish autonomous science across disciplines.

Source check

Abstract; Fig. 1; expert-in-the-loop workflow; real-world validations; code-availability statement.

Kirgis et al., including Narayanan (2026)

Can AI agents conduct open-ended AI research? Early evidence from two case studies

Preprint · arXiv 2607.27191; consulted 9 September 2026

Two shadow evaluations gave agents unpublished AI research questions, six days and substantial compute. Engineering was completed; the original authors judged both papers below the publication bar.

Interpretation: Only two questions; reviews were not blinded. Engineering capability and research judgment are distinct outcomes.

Source check

Primary HTML abstract, sections 3–7 and original-author reviews: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27191v1

Open consulted source

METR (2026)

Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models

Benchmark methodology and updated measurements

Time horizon uses human task duration and a specified probability of agent success on the evaluated task suite.

Interpretation: A 50% horizon is neither uninterrupted agent runtime nor a guarantee of successful week-long research.

Source check

Methodological details and limitations note. No current leaderboard number is reproduced.

A planner, generator and evaluator divide application-building work, with structured artifacts supporting handoffs.
