Working through the argument

Scripted teaching example. These researcher turns are illustrative, not a transcript of Christopher's statements. The source and methods reports linked separately are actual agent reviews.

RESEARCHERI think chatbots may displace friends because they are always available and easier to approach. The finding about heavier users seems consistent with that.

AIIt is consistent with that mechanism. But people who already have less support might also use the chatbot more. What would count as displacement in your argument: fewer requests, fewer responses, or weaker ties?

RESEARCHERFewer requests for help with a personal problem. I am not claiming that fewer requests always mean people are worse off.

AIThen the loneliness findings do not measure your outcome. And the Fang study randomized modes and topics, not minutes of use. We can retain your mechanism as a hypothesis. We need evidence on outreach itself to evaluate it.

RESEARCHERCould we ask people what they did after they needed support, and compare the proportion of needs that led to a friend?

AIThat would be useful descriptively, but there is another issue. If the chatbot changes what people experience or report as a need, the denominator changes. A lower proportion might reflect a different set of episodes. I would make total requests over a fixed follow-up the primary outcome and keep episode sequences as secondary measures.

RESEARCHERI still want to know if the chatbot sometimes helps people reach out. Could we vary whether it suggests contacting a friend?

AIYes. That comparison would estimate the effect of the suggestion feature, relative to the standard chatbot offer. It would not tell us the overall effect of chatbot availability. We can include a usual-access comparison, but because people can use other services, we should describe the main contrast as an effect of offering this chatbot.

RESEARCHERLet's write that narrower study. Leave the population and the smallest effect worth detecting as questions for us to resolve.

AII will revise the outcome and estimands, keep non-users in their assigned groups, and separate missing diaries from zero requests. The protocol will mark the unresolved choices. I will also ask the source reviewer to check that its introduction does not present the motivating association as a causal result.
