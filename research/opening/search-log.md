Follow-up inspection used the SAgE publication list to reach the original CRUX evaluation and paper; Google's announcement to reach its Nature article; author/repository pages to retrieve cognition papers; and METR's own definitions and limitations.

Selection decisions:
- Use Risko and Gilbert as the conceptual anchor. Retain Sparrow as further reading.
- Put Lee and Bastani on the main slide because their designs illustrate different inferential claims. Verify the PNAS correction: it changes an author affiliation, not the reported findings.
- Retain the EEG essay-writing preprint as a discussion link, not the principal causal evidence.
- Contrast expert-assisted biomedical hypothesis evaluation with CRUX's open-ended AI research tasks. Do not describe these as a direct performance comparison.
- Use METR to define time horizons without reproducing a volatile model leaderboard or equating benchmark duration with research autonomy.
- Use Anthropic technical reports to support the architecture explanation, clearly distinguished from peer-reviewed outcome evidence.
- An August 2026 Co-Scientist extension (arXiv:2608.26701) surfaced. The deck anchors its claim in the peer-reviewed Nature paper rather than adding another preprint to an already short opening.

Access and limits are recorded per source in sources.json. PubMed's full page for Sparrow presented a CAPTCHA; the original abstract and Columbia's author account supported the limited statement used. One direct request for the Risko author PDF failed; the UCL repository abstract and indexed manuscript record were available. No quotations or full third-party papers are redistributed.

Interpretation checks: offloading is not synonymous with permanent loss; self-report is not measured skill decay; a lab hypothesis is not a clinical outcome; engineering completion is not a successful research finding; more agents do not make a consensus independent evidence. The 2025/2026 drawings represent the workshop's changing focus. Agents and harnesses existed before 2026.
