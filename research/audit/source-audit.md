# Independent source audit

Reviewed 9 September 2026. Materials: the nine-record evidence table, search log, and cached full text of Fang et al., arXiv:2503.17473v2. Other studies below were assessed from the supplied extraction records, not independently reread in full. This is an actual independent review; no researcher replies are represented.

**Claim under review:** AI chatbot use reduces support-seeking from friends and family.

The supplied evidence does not establish that claim. It leaves a plausible research question, with several distinct mechanisms still open.

1. **The prominent randomized study does not randomize amount of use.** Fang et al. assign modality and conversation topic in nine conditions; everyone is asked to use a chatbot. There is no no-chatbot arm. Their duration analysis therefore cannot identify either the effect of chatbot access or the effect of an extra minute of use. Condition-centering duration removes condition means, not selection within conditions. See [design paragraph p5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2#p5.1), [duration analysis S0.SSx2.p3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2#S0.SSx2.p3.1), and the authors’ explicit acknowledgment of unmanipulated duration and possible reverse causality at [S0.SSx2.p6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2#S0.SSx2.p6.1).

2. **Its socialization outcome is adjacent to the proposed outcome.** The study uses the six-item Lubben Social Network Scale. That is not an episode-level record of initiating a request for help. A person can retain a network yet stop approaching it about particular problems. See [Methods, Socialization, Sx2.SSx1.SSSx8.p3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2#Sx2.SSx1.SSSx8.p3.1). Preserve the instrument name in the evidence table; “socialization” alone hides this measurement decision.

3. **Null condition effects are not evidence of no displacement.** The [v2 abstract](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v2#abstract1.1) reports no significant assigned-condition effects. Those contrasts concern modes/topics, not access versus no access. Nor does nonsignificance demonstrate equivalence. The version should remain explicit when comparing the paper with contemporaneous reporting.

4. **Relief, preferences, and behavior must remain separate.** In the supplied records, De Freitas et al. study loneliness; Young et al. evaluate supplied responses; Meng et al. study support-message production. Each could motivate a mechanism. None, as extracted, demonstrates fewer requests to an existing friend. The evidence table should not count them as three replications of displacement.

5. **The collection cannot establish a literature-wide absence.** The search log describes a purposive teaching sample, selected partly for readability, with no subscription-database search. Two records rely on abstracts. Say “none of the studies retained here directly estimates this outcome,” rather than “no study has tested it.” Ueda’s professional-help attitudes remain a boundary example, outside the main synthesis.

## Questions that change the next research decision

- Is the outcome fewer requests in total, or less human contact *given an episode of need*? If chatbot use changes perceived need, restricting analysis to post-treatment need episodes may select different people or episodes across arms. Which quantity do you want to estimate?
- What observation would count against your substitution argument? For example, would increased friend contact within 24 hours after chatbot use support a bridging account, and would your data distinguish a chatbot-initiated suggestion from a contact that was already planned?

I would retain substitution as a hypothesis, specify those outcomes before analysis, and collect timing and sequence information. The present collection supports that design work; it does not supply the causal conclusion.

## Cross-check of the methods review

I read the independent methods agent’s proposed three-arm pilot. I agree with its decision to make a fixed-window count of requests the primary outcome and treat need-conditional rates as secondary. That answers my concern about selecting on needs reported after assignment. Its offer-versus-usual-access interpretation also avoids claiming to estimate an effect of universal chatbot availability.

One issue remains: the proposed bridge feature is bundled with whatever additional attention, wording, or reminders it entails. Comparing the two chatbot offers identifies the effect of that feature package, not necessarily a psychological bridging mechanism. The protocol should record exactly what differs and distinguish an intervention effect from an explanation of how it works. Sequence diaries can help describe possible pathways, but they do not resolve mediator confounding. I would ask the researcher whether the immediate goal is to evaluate a useful feature or adjudicate the bridging mechanism; the latter requires further design work.
